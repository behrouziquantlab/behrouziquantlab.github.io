What 12 months of data say about MACD divergence

We tested regular and hidden divergence on three FX pairs. On its own, it is a coin flip at 1R — here is why that is useful to know.

Divergence is one of the most quoted ideas in technical analysis: price makes a new extreme, momentum does not, so the move is "tired". We wanted numbers, not anecdotes.

Setup: EURAUD, GBPUSD and USDJPY, M5 and M1, twelve months of closed bars. Every confirmed regular and hidden divergence from both MACD engines (3,5,2 and 12,26,9) was entered at the confirmation bar, with the stop at the pivot and a target of one risk unit.

Result: roughly 50% of trades reached 1R before the stop — the same as a coin flip. The fast engine was 49.2%, the standard engine 50.1%. Both halves of the year agreed.

What it means: divergence is a description of market structure, not a trading edge by itself. Its value is as context — where in a correction you are, whether momentum agrees with the setup you already trade. That is how our own systems use it, and why our indicator never claims profitability.
