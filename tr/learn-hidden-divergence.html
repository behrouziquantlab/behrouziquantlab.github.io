Hidden divergence: continuation inside a trend

A higher low in price with a lower low in momentum — a real example, step by step.

Contents

The ideaReading the exampleCommon mistakes

The idea

In an up-trend, a pull-back holds a higher low while MACD or stochastic makes a lower low. Momentum over-shot, yet buyers defended a higher price. It argues for continuation — only inside an existing trend.

Reading the example

  1. Context: M15 MACD above its signal and M5 EMA 20 above EMA 50 — an up-trend.
  2. Pull-back: M1 drops until K30 is below 20, making a low (HL 1).
  3. Second pull-back: price holds a higher low (HL 2) while MACD dips lower — hidden bullish divergence.
  4. Trigger: a closed bullish candle above the previous high with fast MACD (3,5,2) crossing above zero. Entry on the next open, stop below the low.

Common mistakes

  • Using it against the higher-timeframe trend.
  • Counting tiny differences between pivots as divergence.
  • Entering before the trigger candle closes.
