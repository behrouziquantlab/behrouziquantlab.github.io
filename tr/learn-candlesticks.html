Open, high, low and close — what one candle can and cannot tell you.

Anatomy of a candle

A candle summarises one period: where price opened, the highest and lowest traded prices, and where it closed. The body spans open to close; the wicks show the extremes that did not hold.

  • A long body with small wicks: one side controlled the whole period.
  • A long lower wick: price was pushed down and bought back — rejection of lower prices.
  • A small body with long wicks on both sides: indecision.

Context beats patterns

A hammer in the middle of nowhere means little. The same candle at a tested support, after a pull-back inside an up-trend, carries information. Always ask where a candle formed before asking what it is.

Closed candles only

A candle is not finished until its period closes. A "pin bar" at 12:04 can become a full bearish candle by 12:05. Professional tools — including ours — decide only on closed candles.

Note On M1 and M5, spreads and news spikes create wicks that mean nothing. Higher timeframes filter that noise.

Practise

Open a M15 chart, hide all indicators, and describe the last 20 candles in one sentence each: who controlled the period, and where it happened.
