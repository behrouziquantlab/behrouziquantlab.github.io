Regular divergence: exhaustion, measured

Price makes a new extreme, momentum does not. How to define it so it can be tested — and what the data says.

Contents

The definition we useThe confirmation delay is unavoidableWhat 12 months of data say

The definition we use

  • Regular bearish (RS): price makes a higher high, MACD makes a lower high.
  • Regular bullish (RB): price makes a lower low, MACD makes a higher low.

Both pivots must be confirmed swings, and differences smaller than a tolerance (0.1 × ATR for price, 0.1 × MACD volatility for momentum) count as "equal" — no signal.

The confirmation delay is unavoidable

The second pivot is only known after price moves away from it. A tool that shows the divergence at the exact top in real time is redrawing history. Good tools show when the signal became known.

What 12 months of data say

On EURAUD, GBPUSD and USDJPY, entering at confirmation with the stop at the pivot reached 1R about 50% of the time. Regular divergence is a description of momentum, not an edge on its own. It earns its place as context: at a higher-timeframe zone, after a liquidity sweep, inside a plan.
