Stochastic and the K-lines (K15, K30, K60)

Where the close sits inside the recent range — a clean way to measure pull-back depth.

Contents

The formula

%K = (close − lowest low of N bars) ÷ (highest high − lowest low) × 100. A reading of 10 means price closed near the bottom of the last N bars; 90 near the top.

Three windows, three questions

  • K15: where is price inside the very recent swing?
  • K30: the workhorse — is the current pull-back deep enough to matter?
  • K60: the broader context of the move.

Oversold is not a buy signal

In a strong down-trend K30 can stay below 20 for hours. The useful pattern is a pull-back within an up-trend that drives K30 below 20 and then turns up on a closed candle — exactly the condition many of our setups require.

Stochastic divergence

If price makes a higher low while %K makes a lower low, momentum over-shot during a pull-back — a hidden bullish divergence on the stochastic. Several K-windows agreeing is stronger evidence than one.
