MACD, properly understood

Two exponential averages, their difference and its average — and the settings that change everything.

Contents

How MACD is built

MACD line = EMA(fast) − EMA(slow) of the close. Signal line = an average of the MACD line. Histogram = MACD − signal. The classic setting is 12, 26, 9 (Gerald Appel, late 1970s).

What each part says

  • MACD above zero: the fast average is above the slow one — upward momentum.
  • MACD crossing its signal: momentum is accelerating or decelerating.
  • The histogram's slope turns before the cross — that is why traders watch it for early warnings.

Fast versus standard settings

A fast MACD such as 3, 5, 2 reacts to small swings and is ideal for timing on M1/M5. The standard 12, 26, 9 describes the larger leg. Using both, independently, separates short-term noise from the momentum of the move.

Limits

MACD is derived from price; it adds no new information, only a different view of momentum. It lags, and in ranges it whipsaws. Treat it as a measuring instrument, not an oracle.
