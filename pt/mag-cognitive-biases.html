Seven cognitive biases every trader should recognise

Predictable errors with names — knowing them is the first defence.

The list

  1. Loss aversion — losses hurt more than gains please.
  2. Disposition effect — selling winners early, riding losers.
  3. Overconfidence — overestimating the precision of your analysis; linked to over-trading.
  4. Confirmation bias — seeking evidence that supports the trade you already want.
  5. Recency bias — over-weighting the last few trades.
  6. Hindsight bias — "it was obvious" after the fact, which destroys honest review.
  7. Anchoring — fixating on an entry price or an old high.

The antidote: process

A checklist, a journal with the reason before the outcome, and statistics over many trades. Biases thrive on single, memorable events; they weaken when you look at the whole sample.

Sources & further reading

  1. Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk — Kahneman & Tversky, Econometrica (1979)
  2. The Disposition to Sell Winners Too Early and Ride Losers Too Long — Shefrin & Statman, Journal of Finance (1985)
  3. Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth — Barber & Odean, Journal of Finance (2000)
