Hidden divergence, explained without the myths

A higher low in price with a lower low in momentum: what it shows, and what it does not.

Regular divergence looks for exhaustion. Hidden divergence looks for continuation: in an uptrend, price pulls back to a higher low while momentum dips to a lower low.

The momentum over-shoot shows the pull-back was sharp, yet buyers defended a higher price. It is a statement about the pull-back, not a forecast.

Tolerances matter. Tiny differences between two pivots are noise. Our engine ignores price differences under 0.1 × ATR and MACD differences under 0.1 × its own volatility.

Use hidden divergence where the higher-timeframe structure already supports the trend, and size every position from the stop distance.
