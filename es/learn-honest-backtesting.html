Honest backtesting: no look-ahead, no repaint

Most beautiful backtests are wrong for the same three reasons. How to avoid them.

Contents

1. Look-ahead2. Repainting3. Over-fittingA checklist

1. Look-ahead

Any use of information that was not available at decision time: the current higher-timeframe bar, a pivot confirmed later, the day's close. Record for every signal the bar time, the time the information became available, and the time it was processed.

2. Repainting

Indicators that move past signals as new bars arrive. Test: run the tool on history, save its signals, replay the same data bar by bar and compare. Any difference is repainting.

3. Over-fitting

Trying many parameter sets and keeping the best. Defend against it with an out-of-sample period, a pre-registered rule for what counts as "proven" (for example ≥200 trades and a confidence interval above break-even), and by publishing failures.

Note We hold our own research to these rules — including the results that did not work. See the Research Center.

A checklist

  • Signals on closed bars only.
  • Entry at the next bar's open, not the signal bar's close.
  • Costs included.
  • Results split in halves; both must agree.
