Fear and greed are not opposites — they are the same mistake

Both are reactions to an outcome that has not happened yet. Behavioural finance explains why they feel so strong.

Loss aversion

Kahneman and Tversky showed that losses weigh roughly twice as much as equal gains. In trading this produces two familiar errors: closing winners too early (fear of giving back profit) and holding losers too long (hope of avoiding a realised loss).

The disposition effect

Shefrin and Statman named this pattern the disposition effect in 1985, and it has been found in investor data around the world. It is not a lack of knowledge; it is how human preferences work.

What actually helps

  • Decide exit rules before entry, and write them down.
  • Use orders, not intentions: a stop-loss and a target placed with the trade.
  • Review closed trades by process quality, not by profit.
The market does not know your entry price. Only you do.

Sources & further reading

  1. Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk — Kahneman & Tversky, Econometrica (1979)
  2. The Disposition to Sell Winners Too Early and Ride Losers Too Long — Shefrin & Statman, Journal of Finance (1985)
